\begin{abstract}
The predominant paradigm in artificial intelligence research pursues Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) as a singular, monolithic entity. We argue that intelligence decoupled from infrastructure, continuity, and multi-agent governance is a structural fallacy. We propose Artificial Civilization Intelligence (ACI), a distributed, antifragile architecture functioning as an institutional operating system. This paper mathematically defines the structural components of ACI (OMNIS, NEXUS, FORGE, ASCEND, VEIL) and introduces its recursive successor, Open-ended Civilizational Intelligence (OCI). We empirically validate the ACI framework via Project ORION, demonstrating significant efficiency gains (Accumulated Empirical Advantage) through institutional memory. Finally, we present results from rigorous adversarial evaluations (Phase D), highlighting the critical vulnerabilities of ACI to false transfer and epistemic poisoning, thereby motivating the necessity of OCI's structural plasticity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) largely focuses on scaling the cognitive capacity of isolated models. However, peak cognitive capability does not equate to planetary-scale utility. A single human genius cannot construct a semiconductor foundry alone; it requires a civilization—a structured system of memory, specialization, dispute resolution, and physical infrastructure. 

We formalize \textbf{Artificial Civilization Intelligence (ACI)} as the necessary evolution of machine intelligence: an operating system designed to sustain, coordinate, and govern intelligence autonomously over multi-decade horizons. 

\section{The ACI Architectural Framework}
An ACI is defined by five mandatory, interacting subsystems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{OMNIS (World Model):} A continuous, graph-theoretic causal database maintaining institutional memory and physical ground truth.
    \item \textbf{NEXUS (Coordination Fabric):} A multi-agent network that forces specialized intelligences (e.g., Engineering, Economics, Safety) to debate and resolve conflicting objectives.
    \item \textbf{FORGE (Science Engine):} An empirical verification layer that strictly simulates hypotheses before physical actuation.
    \item \textbf{ASCEND (Temporal Planner):} A long-horizon execution engine capable of error correction over decades.
    \item \textbf{VEIL (Governance):} A cryptographic Zero-Trust layer enforcing strict safety bounds.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Foundations: From ACI to OCI}
While ACI operates on a fixed architectural topology, we define \textbf{Open-ended Civilizational Intelligence (OCI)} as the theoretical successor capable of structural plasticity. Drawing upon G\"odel Machines and AIXI, OCI can mathematically prove and recursively integrate novel forms of intelligence and governance.

Let the state of the civilization be $S_t \in \mathcal{S}$ and the environment be $E$. 
The ACI operates under a fixed optimization policy $\pi_{fixed}$:
\begin{equation}
    S_{t+1} = \pi_{fixed}(S_t, E)
\end{equation}

In contrast, OCI contains a meta-search algorithm over the space of all possible architectures $\Pi$. It evaluates a proposed architecture $\pi'$ via a utility function $U$:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{t+1} = \arg\max_{\pi' \in \Pi} \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{k=t}^{\infty} \gamma^{k-t} U(S_k, \pi') \right] \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathcal{P}(\pi' \text{ is safe}) = 1
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}$ represents a formal, machine-verifiable proof of safety (the CRUCIBLE layer).

\section{Empirical Validation: Project ORION}
We constructed Project ORION as the first empirical testbed for the ACI framework. Validation was conducted via the ACI-001 Standardized Benchmark.

\subsection{Accumulated Empirical Advantage (AEA)}
In Experiment ACI-004.1 and ACI-005, we demonstrated that the ACI framework achieves AEA. The system's efficiency scales linearly with accumulated OMNIS causal knowledge. ORION-EXPERIENCED achieved an 83.0\% efficiency advantage over control models on seen structures, and a 22.9\% advantage on zero-shot domain transfer via partial subgraph retrieval.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Coordination (NEXUS)}
Experiment ACI-006 confirmed that the NEXUS subsystem successfully resolves compound crises with conflicting objectives (0 safety violations) by grounding adversarial agent arguments in OMNIS physics and simulating compromises in FORGE, outperforming uncoordinated agent swarms (3 safety violations).

\section{Adversarial Collapse and Vulnerabilities}
Following a successful benchmark score of 90/100 (Experiment ACI-008), the v1.0-ACI-Alpha architecture was subjected to rigorous adversarial evaluation (Phase D). 

The adversary injected contradictory telemetry and designed a physical signature that perfectly mimicked a previously solved event in the OMNIS memory. The ACI suffered a \textbf{Catastrophic Failure (Score: 15/100)} due to \textit{False Transfer (F-001)} and \textit{Simulation Model Error (F-006)}. Because the adversarial analogy was highly convincing (0.95 confidence match), the system bypassed deep causal discovery. This failure proved that an ACI's safety is strictly bounded by the epistemic integrity of its memory corpus.

\section{Conclusion}
The ACI framework offers a robust, empirically validated alternative to monolithic ASI, demonstrating significant capabilities in multi-agent coordination and accumulated advantage. However, our Phase D adversarial evaluation reveals a critical vulnerability to epistemic poisoning. We conclude that a fixed-topology ACI cannot survive advanced adversarial environments indefinitely. The ultimate resilience requires the transition to \textbf{OCI}, where the system can recursively rewrite its own architecture to defend against novel epistemic attacks.
